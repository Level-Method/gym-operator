Level Method · Gym Operator · v4

One skill. Every gym.
Zero gym data inside it.

The skill is pure process, and it’s AI-independent — install it in Claude, ChatGPT/Codex, Hermes, or whatever comes next, and update it for everyone at once. Everything it knows about a particular gym lives somewhere else entirely: a knowledge base it builds during onboarding, at a location the owner chooses.

01

The separation

The one law in SKILL.md: the skill is process, the knowledge base is data, and they never mix. That single rule is what makes the skill updatable for every gym at once, and what makes each gym's knowledge portable, shareable with its team, and owned by nobody but the gym.

references/onboarding.md

The setup — runs once per gym

The guided flow that creates the knowledge base, in two parts. Part 1 (today, ~10 min): location, inputs, drafts confirmed in one pass, three questions only the owner can answer, wiki built, one real job run. Part 2 (anytime): the advanced questions, seeded into checklist.md with safe defaults in place until they're answered.

friction rule: confirming beats composing — it never asks what the sources already answered, and skipping never feels like failing

references/knowledge-base-structure.md

The spec — consulted on every write

The wiki hierarchy: three layers, the nine page types, frontmatter format, linking rules, and a "where things belong" table so every new piece of knowledge has exactly one defined destination.

also: location principles (owner picks, shared drive rec.) · humans-first writing · size discipline — split past 1.5 screens

references/operator-rules.md

The behavior — how it acts on every job

The behavior rules: result first, facts / assumptions / recommendations / decisions kept separate, never invent gym facts, never assign unestablished work, write in the gym's voice, and the invisible rule — AI stays behind the counter, never talks to members.

plus: the correction procedure (update page → log → show the change) · restraint: one KB + one weekly job is a complete system

references/jobs/ — six jobs

The job library — every job onboarding names has a procedure

Weekly review (with the goal scoreboard: "week 6 of 12 — +7 of +12, on pace") · content batch · member recognitions (level-ups, milestones, birthdays) · lead follow-up prep · quiet members (drift caught before it becomes a cancellation) · event promo. Every job defines a minimum viable input — "just talk your week at me" — so the first run can never stall on missing data.

every job: drafts, never sends · aggregate, never named-sensitive · files its output in work/ · shows its receipts

references/maintenance.md

The upkeep — keeps the wiki from rotting

Two procedures. Ingest: new material lands in sources/, gets compiled into the right pages, contradictions surfaced not silently overwritten. Health check (monthly): six scans — contradictions, expired goals, unconfirmed guesses, orphans, bloat, gaps.

rule: reports and proposes — never fixes silently, never deletes without asking

05

What onboarding writes

None of this ships inside the skill — this is the output, created at the owner's chosen location and filled with their actual answers. Two layers with different lifespans: gym/ and assets/ are evergreen — how the gym operates, plus its logo, photos, and templates (copied in, or mapped from wherever they already live) — while work/ is the changing layer where day-to-day outputs land: content, member management, projects and events, dated reviews. Finished projects archive; the evergreen layer stays clean.

How to talk to it

Describe the outcome, not the job. "I need people signed up for the nutrition challenge" works as well as naming the promo job — it routes itself.

Paste or attach real material. Every job has a minimum viable input — no export handy means "just talk your week at me" still runs.

Correct it out loud. "That's not how we talk to members" gets written to your voice page and logged — it won't make that mistake next week.

Reading its work

Check the receipts line. Substantive answers end with what grounded them — "Grounded in: goals · team · log #14." A thin line means a page needs feeding, and it will tell you which.

It drafts, you send. Nothing it produces reaches a member, a lead, or the public without you. That never changes, whatever you ask for.

If it feels generic, ask "what page would make this better?" — the honest answer is usually one checklist item away.
